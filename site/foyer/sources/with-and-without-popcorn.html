The difference is a witness.

An agreement can be clear and still become arguable later. POPCORN does one narrow job: it supplies independent, checkable evidence about a record’s fingerprint and timing.

Without POPCORN

The timing record depends on what already exists.

  1. Terms are agreed.

    A document, message, or task record says what each side expects.

  2. A threshold is crossed.

    A date is held, work begins, access changes, or payment becomes due.

  3. The question comes later.

    Was this the same version? Did it exist before the threshold? Which clock are we trusting?

  4. Evidence is pieced together.

    Screenshots, chat history, platform timestamps, and memory each tell part of the story.

Result: without an independent witness, the timing question can remain disputed.
With POPCORN

The record can be checked.

  1. Terms are agreed.

    The exact document, message, or task record gets a collision-resistant fingerprint.

  2. The fingerprint is witnessed.

    The POPCORN node returns a signed receipt stating the interval in which it received that fingerprint.

  3. A threshold is crossed.

    The commitment and its authorization still belong to the participants.

  4. The receipt is verified.

    Anyone with the record, receipt, and accepted witness key can check the match locally.

Result: the timing evidence is checkable without asking the record to remember itself.

The line matters.

Not every interaction needs witnessing. The useful moment is the commitment point—the place where crossing the line creates a consequence.

A SHA-256 fingerprint lets exact bytes be compared. Change the record—even slightly—and the fingerprint is overwhelmingly likely to change with it.

A match verifies these bytes against the recorded fingerprint—not authorship or time. A POPCORN receipt adds the witness’s signed interval for when that fingerprint was received.

A narrow claim is a strong claim.

POPCORN can show

  • A specific fingerprint reached the witness during a stated interval.
  • The receipt verifies under the witness key the verifier accepts.
  • The record’s bytes still match—or no longer match—the recorded fingerprint.

POPCORN does not show

  • Who agreed, who had authority, or whether approval was still valid.
  • Who authored the record, whether work was complete, or whether terms were good.
  • What consequence should follow. Each participant decides that for itself.

The clock witnesses. It doesn’t decide.
